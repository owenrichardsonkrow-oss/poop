## Decision 1 — T-side default style

Driven by the initiative count.

| Initiative 4–5 players | What to write |
|---|---|
| 2 or more | Map-control defaults with genuine duel-taking. Contact plays, fast follow-ups, aggressive map presence. Your space-takers can open rounds on their own. |
| Exactly 1 | One designated entry, everything else built to support him. Do not write plays that need two simultaneous openings — you cannot staff them. |
| 0 | Do **not** write entry-dependent strats. Build around utility executes and picks: get the opening kill without anyone face-checking. Slow defaults, heavy util, trade off the pick. |

## Decision 2 — Execute style

Driven by aim shapes.

- Mostly **close-range spray** players → short-angle hits, tight executes,
  flashes over smokes, get bodies into the site fast.
- Mostly **mid-range tap** players → long-angle control, mid-map presence,
  pick-oriented rounds, smokes to cut sightlines rather than flashes to rush.
- Mixed → write both, and match the play to who is alive and where.

## Decision 3 — Complexity budget

**Your stratbook is capped by your least disciplined player, not your
average.** A play that four people execute correctly and one person
improvises through is not a play, it is a coin flip that costs you utility.

| Lowest discipline score | Budget |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Very few set plays. Simple triggers. Loose defaults with clear individual responsibilities. Spend practice time on utility habits before adding strats. |
| 3 | A modest book — perhaps two executes per site per map, plus defaults. |
| 4–5 | You can run a thick book: multiple executes, mid-round calls off triggers, set retakes. |

Decision:

## Decision 4 — CT structure

Anchors are the scarcest reliable resource on most rosters. Assign them
first, then build the rest of the CT side around how many you have.

- Assign anchors from the **low-initiative / holding-shape** players.
- Count how many you have:
  - **2+ anchors** → hold-heavy setups are viable. Play for the pick, retake
    only when necessary.
  - **1 anchor** → he takes the harder site. Everything else plays for
    information and rotation speed.
  - **0 anchors** → retake-heavy CT. Do not ask aggressive players to sit
    still for 40 seconds; they will not, and the setup will break anyway.
    Give them aggression with a purpose instead.

## Decision 5 — AWP

- If you have a real AWPer (meaningful AWP round share, mid-range shape),
  build T-side picks and CT-side long control around him.
- If nobody AWPs, do not force it in week one. An unpracticed AWP is worse
  than a fifth rifle, and it drains the economy.

## Decision 6 — Veto

Driven by map pool overlap, not by individual comfort.

- Permaban: the map fewest players are comfortable on.
- Priority picks: the maps 4+ players are comfortable on.
- Note any map where your **only** anchor or **only** AWPer is weak — that is
  a hidden veto liability.

## Open questions for the first meeting

Ask these before showing anyone their profile. Compare their answers to the
data privately afterwards.

2. Which maps do you actually want to play?
3. What do you need from an IGL — how much do you want called?
4. Do you want to entry? (Ask separately from question 1. The answers differ.)
5. Who have you played with before?
